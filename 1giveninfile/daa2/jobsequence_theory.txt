## Job Sequencing with Deadlines — Theory

### 1. Definition
Job Sequencing with Deadlines is a **Greedy Algorithm** problem in which each job has a **deadline** and a **profit**. Every job requires exactly **one unit of time**. The objective is to select and arrange jobs so that the **total profit is maximum**.

### 2. Basic Conditions
- Each job takes one unit of time.
- A job earns its profit only when completed **on or before its deadline**.
- Only one job can be executed at a time.
- A job cannot be divided.
- The goal is to maximize total profit.

### 3. Greedy Approach
First, all jobs are sorted according to their **profit in decreasing order**. We consider the job with the highest profit first because our objective is to maximize profit.

After selecting a job, we place it in the **latest available time slot before its deadline**.

### 4. Why Latest Slot?
Suppose a job has deadline 3. We first try slot 3, then slot 2, and finally slot 1.

Placing it as late as possible keeps the earlier slots available for jobs having smaller deadlines.

### 5. Example

| Job | Deadline | Profit |
|---|---:|---:|
| J1 | 2 | 100 |
| J2 | 1 | 50 |
| J3 | 2 | 20 |
| J4 | 1 | 70 |
| J5 | 3 | 60 |

After sorting by profit:

```text
J1 → J4 → J5 → J2 → J3
```

Available slots:

```text
1    2    3
_    _    _
```

Schedule:

```text
J4   J1   J5
```

Total profit:

\[
70 + 100 + 60 = 230
\]

Therefore, the **maximum profit is 230**.

### 6. Algorithm Steps

1. Store each job with its ID, deadline and profit.
2. Sort jobs in decreasing order of profit.
3. Find the maximum deadline.
4. Create empty time slots.
5. Take each job one by one.
6. Search from its deadline towards slot 1.
7. Place the job in the first available slot.
8. If no slot is available, skip the job.
9. Add the profit of all scheduled jobs.

### 7. Complexity

For the implementation we used:

- **Time Complexity:** `O(n²)`
- **Space Complexity:** `O(n)`

### 8. Important Viva Points

**Why is it greedy?**  
Because we make the locally best choice by considering the highest-profit job first.

**Why sort by profit?**  
To give priority to jobs that provide higher profit.

**Why schedule from deadline backwards?**  
To place the job as late as possible and preserve earlier slots.

**What if the deadline is 3?**  
The job can be placed in slot 1, 2, or 3.

**Can two jobs occupy the same slot?**  
No, only one job can be executed in one time slot.

**Maximum number of jobs?**  
If the maximum deadline is `D`, at most `D` jobs can be scheduled.
